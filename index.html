About

My research examines the dynamics of inflation across advanced and emerging economies, spanning its cyclical, structural, and policy dimensions. The work sits at the intersection of monetary and international economics: it uses structural VARs and local projections to empirically identify the drivers of inflation, and open-economy DSGE models to theoretically test them, across both country groups.

Separately, I have worked on structural trade models to examine the effects of trade agreements, and used input-output analysis to estimate the size of India's digital economy. Going forward, I plan to extend this research using Heterogeneous Agent New Keynesian (HANK) models and Bayesian econometric methods to better capture how household heterogeneity shapes the transmission of inflation and monetary policy.

Job Market Paper

Two-Channel Transmission of Global Inflation Shock

Arham Nahar
Abstract

This paper examines the two-channel transmission of a global inflation shock to CPI inflation in emerging economies.A high frequency external instrument identification strategy, following \cite{Kanzig}, is employed to empirically test the impact of the global inflation shock on the relevant nominal exchange rate. Thereafter A three bloc open economy dynamic stochastic general equilibrium (DSGE) model is then constructed to theoretically examine the multi-regional spillover impact of a global inflation shock. The paper finds opposing price pressures emanating from the factors of production, with the cost of composite intermediate goods exerting upward price pressure while downward price pressure is exerted owing to a fall in wages. Evidence is also found for the existence of an indirect channel of the global inflation shock, which operates through changes in the relevant nominal exchange rate following the shock. The indirect channel either amplifies or dampens the direct channel, depending on the direction of the exchange rate change induced by the global inflation shock. The paper further identifies the importance of currency invoicing, the degree of exchange rate pass-through, and trade structure as key channels through which global inflation shocks are transmitted to domestic prices in Emerging Economies.
